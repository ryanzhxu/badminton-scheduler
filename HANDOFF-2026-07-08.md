### What is worth remembering

- The design system is centered on Libre Franklin, Tabler icons, and the Trulioo brand palette.
- The canonical light palette uses dark green, mid green, teal, mint, pale blue, yellow, and the neutral grays already reflected in the current app.
- The dark palette in the design bundle uses a deep green/teal shell with bright aqua primary action color, lighter secondary interactive color, and the same success/warning/error surfaces.
- The top app shell is a sticky 60px header with a centered 916px content area, a Trulioo logo on the left, and icon buttons on the right.
- The header buttons in the reference are theme + language only; QR is handled in the schedule area, not the header.
- The layout language is card-based with 8px radius surfaces, 12- to 16px spacing, and small eyebrow labels in uppercase.
- Court cards use a 3-column body: Team A, `vs`, Team B.
- Team A chips are mint/green; Team B chips are pale blue; singles courts use a teal court header.
- The schedule pane includes round navigation, a sit-out banner, a share QR card, a player stats table, and a constraint validation card.
- The QR/share card in the bundle includes a visible QR canvas, a scan label, a share button, a QR code text label, and a share URL.
- The QR prototype explores four styles: Forest, Rally, Court, and Smash.
- The QR designs all share a central shuttlecock logo and use error-correction level H.
- The prototype language set still includes Thai, but the live app later removed Thai support, so treat that as historical reference only.
- The bundle’s demo helpers, conflict selection chips, and stats/validation copy are all useful as wording references if we need to keep UI language consistent.

### What is historical only

- The old design bundle assumes the settings icon opens a modal and that QR styling is handled in the schedule card.
- It is a design reference, not production code to copy verbatim.
- If we revisit the UI again, use the bundle for spacing, colors, copy, and component shape, but keep the current app’s newer behavior decisions unless the user asks to change them.
